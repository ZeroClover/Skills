# Proximity

Source: Robin Williams, *The Non-Designer's Design Book*, 4th edition, Chapter 2, "Proximity." Items that belong together sit close enough to become one visual unit. The purpose is organization. Organized information is easier to read and easier to remember.

## Rules

Group by meaning. Put the heading, the control, and the text that explains them in one cluster. Separate clusters with a larger gap than the gap inside a cluster.

Let the reader follow one path from a clear start to a clear end. Equal gaps between every item imply that every item has the same relationship. That implication is false when some items are a label and a value and others are a new topic.

Count the groups, not the elements. Squint until details drop out and count where the eye stops. Williams treats roughly three to five stops as a workable page, depending on the piece. More stops means some of those elements should join an existing group.

Keep a heading closer to the content it introduces than to the content above it. The same rule covers captions, prices, button labels, and form hints.

White space is the result of grouping. It marks the boundary between groups. Filling empty corners, or parking an unrelated item in the middle of a group, invents a relationship that the content does not have.

## Examples

Illustrative. Match the structure, not the wording.

**Do.** A product card with the name, price, and Add button in one tight stack, and a wide gap before the next card. The name sits 8 pixels under the photo. The next card starts 32 pixels later. The eye stops once per card.

**Avoid.** The same name, price, and button separated by the same 16-pixel gap used between cards. The price looks as related to the next product as to its own. A second avoid: a phone number tucked into the corner of a poster because the corner was empty, far from the event title it belongs with.

## Review questions

- Which items are one idea, and are they one visual unit?
- Is every heading closer to what it names than to the previous block?
- If you squint, how many times does the eye stop? Which stops can merge?
- Does any gap imply a relationship the content does not have?
- Is anything placed in a corner or in the center only because that spot was empty?
